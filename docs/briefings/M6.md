## 2. How it works

- **Archive.** `SnapshotService.generate()` computes `PerformanceService.compute(...)`, builds the canonical payload (sorted keys, Decimals fixed-string, ISO dates, explicit nulls, no floats) and persists the payload + SHA-256 with its metadata. The payload is the comparison target; nothing mutable participates.
- **Replay vs restatement.** `replay()` recomputes from stored period/K/snapshot metadata only and compares canonical hashes: `REPLAY_EXACT`, `ENGINE_VERSION_MISMATCH`, `NAV_SNAPSHOT_MISMATCH` or `PAYLOAD_MISMATCH`. `restatement(K2)` recomputes at the later knowledge time, emits `RESTATEMENT_GENERATED` (or `NO_CHANGE`) with per-field `{original, restated}` deltas, and never overwrites the archive. Attribution links changes to the corrected revisions' correction type/reason, with the nonlinear-measures limitation from METHODOLOGY §11.
- **Comparator.** One-sided current rows only: corrected transactions are unreproducible (prior amount/lineage/reason overwritten); uncorrected accounts remain reproducible. The report is counts, never a "win %" (D-022).

## 4. Three interviewer questions

**Q1. What is the difference between replay and restatement in one sentence?**
Replay recomputes the *same* inputs and must reproduce the archive byte-for-byte; restatement recomputes with *updated* inputs at a later K and produces a new report without touching the old one.

**Q2. Why is storing the PDF not enough?**
A PDF cannot be recomputed. The snapshot stores the canonical payload plus its hash and all the lineage (snapshot, engine, schema, config, rounding) needed to re-derive the same number deterministically — which is why the hash comparison is a genuine test.

**Q3. What does the overwrite comparator actually prove?**
That once history is overwritten, a corrected account's original numbers cannot be recovered from retained data. It reports exactly which transactions are lost, rather than claiming a percentage improvement.
