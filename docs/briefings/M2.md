## 2. How it works

- **Master data.** Investors (`SYN_INV-0001` …) round-robin own accounts (`SYN_ACC-0001` …). Account status (`ACTIVE`/`CLOSED`), opened date and (if closed) closed date are sampled from the configured ranges. Currency is INR. Every record carries `data_class: synthetic_accounts_real_nav` and the `synthetic_label`.
- **Transactions.** One integer-seeded RNG and sorted iteration only; money sampled in paise (no float). Six approved types only. Each account is funded by an explicit `CONTRIBUTION` (accounts start at zero, D-027). Monthly `SIP_INSTALMENT`s are added in addition to the weighted discretionary slots. Scheme transactions are restricted to NAV-available `effective_date`s (`allotment_date = effective_date`, D-036). `posting_sequence` is assigned per account per effective date (10, 20, 30 …); `recorded_at` is `effective_date` at 18:00 UTC plus a deterministic minute offset (D-038).
- **Feasibility (generator only).** A cash/estimated-holdings walk in posting order sizes redemptions/switches as a sampled fraction of holdings and caps purchases/withdrawals by cash; infeasible samples fall back deterministically to `CONTRIBUTION`/`PURCHASE`. Estimates are labelled `generator_estimated_*` (D-037). M3 recomputes all derived values.
- **Correction intents.** A configurable fraction of eligible transactions receive an unrealised intent (`REPLACE`/`VOID`/`REVERSE_AND_REPLACE`, proposed instruction change, reason code, synthetic operator, `recorded_at` 1–30 days later). Intents are not applied in M2 (D-039); M3 is their consumer.
- **Provenance and hashes.** The manifest records `generator_version`, `config_hash`, `seed`, `nav_snapshot_id`, counts by type, date range, simulated-clock rules, scheme pool and per-list + overall SHA-256 hashes.

## 4. Three interviewer questions

**Q1. Why does the generator not write ledger tables?**
Because the ledger is M3's job. M2 is a pure function (`config × seed × NAV index → records`) so the FR-B1 exit gate — identical hashes from identical inputs — is testable without inventing a ledger schema early. The transaction records are instruction-level intents; derived units/proceeds are recomputed by M3.

**Q2. How do you guarantee a generated withdrawal does not overdraw the account without a ledger?**
Feasibility is enforced *by construction* inside the generator's walk in `posting_sequence` order: purchases/withdrawals are capped by cash, redemptions/switches are sized as a fraction of estimated holdings, and failures fall back deterministically and are counted. Those estimates are labelled as such and are not persisted as financial facts beyond the instruction fields.

**Q3. Why is the simulated clock needed, and why not wall-clock time?**
`recorded_at` is knowledge time. Wall-clock "now" would collapse K1 and K2 and make replay meaningless, and it is not reproducible. The generator's clock (18:00 UTC for transactions, +1–30 days at 11:00 UTC for corrections) is a controlled, reproducible input, which is what makes the M6 K1/K2 restatement scenario testable. The synthetic label and the manifest state that it is a scenario, not a latency claim.

## 5. Limitations for review

- Restrictions (`allotment_date = effective_date`) mean split-date SIP/SWITCH cases are not exercised by the synthetic dataset; they are covered by the `docs/METHODOLOGY.md` fixtures (D-036).
- This seed produced zero `CLOSED` accounts; statuses are sampled probabilistically. A different seed or mix changes this; the manifest reports the actual mix.
- SIP instalments dominate the final counts because they are monthly and additional to the weighted discretionary slots.
- Some correction intents (notably `VOID` of a funding contribution) may legitimately be rejected by M3's validity rule; that is expected, not a generator defect.
- Outputs are written to `output/synthetic/` (git-ignored); the hashes above are the portable review artefact. FundLens was not modified; no push or publication occurred.
